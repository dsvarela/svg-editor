All three landed. The most expensive thing that happened was none of them: a

## The sweep that would not die, and the `pgrep` that said it had

`tools/mutate.mjs` rewrites the file it is measuring. `CLAUDE.md` has warned
about that in two directions since 2026-08-20: nothing may edit `src/` while a
sweep is up, and a sweep will silently revert an edit made under it.

I started one, decided it was too slow to block on, and killed it:

    kill 3854448
    pgrep -c "mutate.mjs" || echo "no mutate running"   # printed 0

**`pgrep` without `-f` matches the process name, and the process name is
`node`.** The pattern `mutate.mjs` appears only in the argument list, so the
check could not have matched a running sweep however many there were. It printed
`0`, I read that as "dead", and the sweep ran for another eleven minutes,
swapping one operator at a time in `src/tools/commands.ts` while I wrote tests
against it.

Three tests failed in that window. Two of the three were the mutation:

| Failure | Cause |
|---|---|
| `Removed 2 guides.` came back wrong | live mutation elsewhere in the file |
| `Removed 1 guides.` for a count of 1 | the same |
| `cutSelection` returned `true` on a locked shape | **real**, and below |

Every green recorded between 02:48 and 02:55 is worth nothing, and everything
was re-run on a still tree afterwards. This is the seventh time a signal in this
project has measured nothing, and the first where the broken instrument was a
shell command rather than a tool in the repository.

**The fix is structural and it is one command.** `code` rule 11 says to make the
class impossible rather than to remember it, and two things writing to one
working tree is a class:

    git worktree add --detach <somewhere> HEAD
    ln -s "$PWD/node_modules" <somewhere>/node_modules
    cd <somewhere> && node tools/mutate.mjs src/tools/commands.ts

The sweep then owns its own checkout. `src/` in the main tree stays editable for
the whole run, the measurement cannot be disturbed by an edit, and an edit cannot
be reverted by the measurement. The worktree costs a symlink and about a second.
Nothing in the repository has to be remembered for this to hold, which is the
difference between a fix and a note.

Its second benefit was not the point and mattered as much: the sweep takes about
three hours at full `related` scope, and with it in a worktree that is three
hours of work rather than three hours of waiting.

### The fixtures that separate one mechanism from another

Four of the tests exist because a simpler fixture would have passed either way,
which is this project's oldest register entry:

- **`insertInSelection` on the pair that wraps.** Nodes 0 and 3 of a closed
  square are neighbours across the closing segment; nodes 0 and 3 of an open
  path of the same shape are two ends with nothing between them. Only
  `sp.closed` separates them, so both fixtures are needed and a version with
  that term deleted passes the first.
- **`selectionCount` on a shape and one of its own nodes.** The method answers by
  two different routes, a sum when only shapes are selected and a deduped list
  once a node is named. A selection that is all shapes or all nodes runs one
  route; the union is the only fixture that can catch the double count.
- **`countNodes` against `countSegments`.** A closed subpath has as many segments
  as nodes. With only closed fixtures the two functions are interchangeable, so
  the document holds one closed shape and one open one.
- **`fitToPixels` at two stroke widths.** The lattice phase is computed from the
  stroke: width 1 wants half pixels, width 2 wants whole ones. A fixture at one
  width cannot tell a computed phase from a constant.

### And two of my own that could not fail

Both were written, both passed, and neither could have distinguished right from
wrong. They are recorded because the failure mode is the one this project keeps
finding and I found it in my own work twice in an hour.

**A curve compared against a cloud of samples.** `insertInSelection` says
`Node inserted, and the curve is unchanged.`, so the test sampled the curve
before and after and asked how far the second strayed from the first. It read
0.167 and I first took that for a defect. It is the measurement's own floor:
`ARC` leaves its first node at three times the handle length, so 200 samples in
`t` sit up to 0.335 apart there and nothing measured against them resolves
better than half of that. **A sampled comparison cannot be sharper than its
sample spacing, and the spacing of a cubic in `t` is not its spacing in space.**
The test compares parameters now, the first half's `t` against `t / 2` of the
original, which is exact and pins the split to the middle at the same time.

**A baseline captured before the displacement it was meant to survive.**
`fitToPixels` carries handles along with the anchors they belong to. The test
recorded each handle's reach from its anchor, then moved the anchor by hand to
put it off the lattice, then fitted. The reach it compared against was the one
from before its own displacement, so the test was asking the fit to undo
something the test had done. Recording the reach after the displacement makes it
a number the fit has to preserve.

## Every sentence the layer writes

29 of the 249 survivors sat inside a message and 64 on the boolean beside one,
because the tests that ran these methods read the document and nothing else.
That is item 2 of the queue, and it needed an instrument rather than a reading:
105 sentences across 58 methods is more than anyone checks by eye.

`scratchpad/msgcheck.mjs` lists every string the class writes, grouped by the
method it sits in, and greps `test/` for each. The first run said **42 of 105
were asserted by nothing**. After the pass it says 5, and all five are
unreachable.

The number in between is the finding.

### The checker was fooled by its own documentation

At one point it printed `0 of 105`. That was wrong, and wrong in the way this
register calls **the number was fine and the condition was not**.

The five unreachable sentences are documented in a comment in
`test/commands.test.ts` that explains, one by one, why nothing can reach them.
That comment quotes each sentence. The checker greps the test tree as text, so
each quoted sentence counted as covered, and **the file explaining why five
sentences have no test is what convinced the checker that all five had one.**

It strips comments from the test corpus before searching now. A check that reads
its own excuses is worse than no check, because it looks like coverage.

### The five that cannot be reached

Each guards an assumption about another module rather than about the caller, so
each stays. What they are not is testable, and a mutation survivor at any of
them is explained rather than open.

| Sentence | Why nothing reaches it |
|---|---|
| `Pick two different nodes.` | needs `fuseNodes` handed one index twice; two distinct node ids never resolve to one position |
| `Nothing to insert into.` | needs `splitSegment` to fail, and every path through it returns `aI + 1` |
| `Those two ends cannot be connected.` | needs `connectEnds` or `mergeEnds` to refuse after `joinSelection`'s guards passed, and every condition they refuse on is one of those guards |
| `Copied, but nothing could be removed.` | needs `deleteSelection` to report zero after a copy succeeded, and `deleteNode` refuses only an index outside the array, which `selectedRefs` cannot produce |
| `That image could not be traced.` | needs `traceImage` to throw on integer work over a raster it has already validated |

The fourth is the one that cost something. The test written for it locked the
shape first, on the assumption that a locked shape copies and refuses to go.
**Locking is not enforced in `Commands` at all** -- `deleteSelection` never reads
`state.locked`, and the refusal lives further out, in what a locked shape lets
you select. The test failed, the failure was real, and reading it is what showed
the branch is unreachable rather than merely untested.

### And one that was reachable and looked like the others

`That outline comes apart; nothing was changed.` needs `strokeOutline` to return
null for a shape that does have a stroke. A six-wide square with a stroke width
of 40 does it: offsetting inward by 20 collapses the inner contour, so there is
no band to make. It is a test now.

## `src/main.ts` has a test

4 019 lines that nothing imports. It runs at module scope against the real
`index.html`, so no unit test could reach it, and the only instrument it has
ever had is somebody reading it. The read on `2026-08-21` found four defects and
would keep finding them one at a time forever, which is why the queue said
**more reading is not what retires this**.

What retires it is a load. `$` is `document.querySelector(sel) as T`, which
hands back `null` for a selector that misses and then throws at the first
`.addEventListener` bound to it. So a renamed id, a deleted control or a handler
wired to a selector that never matched is a `TypeError` at import, and importing
the module against the shipped markup is what catches it.

`test/main.test.ts` reads `index.html` with a `?raw` import, puts its `<body>`
into jsdom, stubs the three browser APIs jsdom lacks (`matchMedia`,
`ResizeObserver`, `URL.createObjectURL`), mocks the Vite worker import, and
imports `src/main.ts`. Seven assertions: the module loads, the starter document
reaches the DOM, no `d` attribute holds `NaN`, a tool button changes which one
reads pressed, a guide placed by number reaches the status line with the right
class, and Group is disabled rather than allowed to refuse.

**Watched fail three ways**, because a smoke test that cannot fail is the worst
of both:

| Break | Result |
|---|---|
| `on('#guideAddV', ...)` renamed to `#guideAddVv` | `TypeError: Cannot read properties of null`, all 7 skipped |
| `commands.onMessage = say` removed | 1 failed |
| the `ok` ternary on the status class inverted | 1 failed |

What it does not do is stand in for a browser scenario. jsdom lays nothing out,
so it proves the app assembles and that its controls answer, and geometry stays
in `npm run drive`.

The first thing it found was that the group button's id is `groupShapes` and the
status line is `#status`, both of which I had guessed wrong from the handler
names. That is the class of defect it exists for, arriving before it had been
committed.

## Two dead statements, and the compiler that could not see them

`rg -n "^\s*void [a-zA-Z_][a-zA-Z0-9_]*;" src/ tools/` returned two, both in
`commands.ts`:

- `groupSelection` declared `let made = ''`, assigned `made = group.id` inside
  the edit, and ended with `void made`. The id was captured and discarded.
- `setSelectedSegmentsCurved` ended with `void s`, where `s` is read four lines
  above.

`tsconfig.json` sets `noUnusedLocals: true`, and **`void x` counts as a read**,
so the compiler could not report either one. Removing both still typechecks,
which is the confirmation: `made` had no other reader. A third instance was
removed from `main.ts` on 2026-08-21, so this is a class with three members and
now none.

`void x` has one honest use, which is discarding a promise. Nothing here was one.

### Reading them found four more, which is the rule working

`2026-08-21b` measured the second read finding six gaps the first had walked
past, over the same three ranges: 51, then 14, then 6. It happened again here,
and the four are worth naming because each is a fixture that could not
distinguish two mechanisms:

| Gap | Why the count hid it |
|---|---|
| `joinSelection`'s free-end guard | the fixture picked two interior nodes, and `\|\|` and `&&` agree when both halves are true |
| `freeActiveSegment`'s displacement | the test asserted the bend was gone, which is true of any displacement, including one across the canvas |
| continuity at the far end of a path | the fixture used node 0, so `r.i === sp.nodes.length - 1` could say anything |

The second is the one to remember. The method exists to lengthen a handle by a
tenth of a percent, and the argument for that number is that it is below what
the eye can see and above what `bendOf` calls symmetric. **Neither half of that
argument was measured.** The test read the consequence, that the segment no
longer reports a bend, and every mutation of the arithmetic that produces it
survived.

All four were fixed, and each was mutated by hand afterwards to watch its test
go red. The two ranges they sit in were re-swept:

| Range | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| `joinSelection`, sites 220-250 | 10 | 9 |
| `freeActiveSegment` and `setSelectedContinuity`, sites 380-425 | 22 | 16 |

figure and 91 is the arithmetic**, and only the two rows above were checked.

### What the 98 mostly are

One class, and it is the same one as the five unreachable sentences: **a null
guard on a lookup that cannot fail.**

```ts
const a = shapeA?.subpaths[ra.sp];
const b = shapeB?.subpaths[rb.sp];
if (!shapeA || !shapeB || !a || !b) return false;
```

Every operator in that condition is a survivor, and so is the `return false`,
because the branch is never taken. `joinSelection` holds nine of them.

They are cheap and they are honest: each says that this method does not trust a
lookup it did not perform itself. What they are not is reachable, and a
mutation testing tool cannot tell a guard that never fires from a guard nobody
wrote a test for. **Deciding what to do about that class is the next question
and not this one**, because the two candidate answers are opposite -- delete
them and let the lookup throw, or keep them and teach the tool to skip an
unreachable branch -- and picking between them wants an argument, not a
count.
