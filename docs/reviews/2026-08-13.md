# Review, 2026-08-13

Covers everything built since [2026-08-12b](2026-08-12b.md): knot removal and the
Simplify redesign, Make one shape and Split into shapes, direct curve dragging,
keylines, and rulers with guides. Eleven feature commits, 26 files, about 4 000
lines.

The three places picked to look hardest at were chosen before anything was read,
on the grounds that they took repeated and uncoordinated change: `model/snapping.ts`
was modified three times, `Store`'s snapshot gained its first new field since the
backdrop, and the shell layout moved the drawing surfaces into a `.stage` cell
under all 32 browser scenarios.

**What it found: 6 defects, 4 documentation claims that were false or unverifiable,
and 2 tests that could not fail.** Everything below was fixed in the same pass
unless it says otherwise.

---

## Defects

### 1. Three guide gestures each left an undo step that undoes nothing

`Store.edit` checkpoints first and asks questions later, which is right for a
drag and wrong for a press that turns out to change nothing. `tryEdit` exists
precisely for that, and the guide gestures did not use it. Three ways in:

| Gesture | What it recorded |
|---|---|
| Press a ruler where a guide already sits | An entry, from the refused `addGuide` |
| Drag a guide out and drop it straight back off the canvas | An entry that undoes to no guide, which is where it already was |
| Press an existing guide and release without moving it | An entry, from a `settleGuide` that settled nothing |

All three also threw the redo stack away, since `take` clears it before the
mutation runs. Pressing a dead ruler five times cost five presses of `Ctrl+Z`,
none of which visibly did anything -- which is the exact complaint `tryEdit` was
written to answer, arriving again through a door it did not cover.

Fixed by using `tryEdit` for both the creation and the settle, and by treating a
newly-born guide dropped straight back as an **abandoned gesture** rather than a
reversed edit: `rollback`, the same as any other drag that decides against
itself. `test/guides-controller.test.ts` is new and covers all three, plus the
two cases that *should* record a step.

Accepted, not fixed: an abandoned gesture still loses the redo stack, because
`rollback` does not restore it. That is true of every gesture in the controller,
not just guides, so it is one finding about `rollback` rather than one about
guides, and it is left for a review that can look at all of them.

### 2. The vertical ruler measured itself against the horizontal span

`rulerTicksFor` took the whole camera and read `camera.w`. The horizontal ruler
wants `camera.w` against the stage width; the vertical one wants `camera.h`
against the stage height. Handing both the width makes the vertical ruler's step
wrong by the aspect ratio.

It survived a screenshot because the 1-2-5 ladder quantises: on a 1290 by 772
stage the two agree at most zooms. They first part at a camera 129 units wide,
where the left ruler labels every 2 and the grid draws every 1.

Fixed by taking a span and a length rather than a camera, so each strip is
measured against its own axis.

### 3. Ruler ticks were finer than the grid they claimed to borrow

`rulerTicksFor` passed its own `minPx` of 7 into `gridDisplayFor`, whose default
is 9. So the ruler asked for a finer lattice than the one being drawn and got
one: at a snap step of 3 on a 4000-unit camera, ticks every 30 against grid lines
every 60. Half the ticks stood over nothing.

Fixed by defaulting to the grid's own 9 and passing it through, so agreement
holds by construction rather than by coincidence.

### 4. `Lock guides` also stopped new ones being made

The ruler refused to hand out a guide while guides were locked, so the strip
looked broken while the **Vertical** and **Horizontal** buttons an inch away
carried on working. Lock is about the guides on the canvas -- do not move what is
already there -- not about the ruler.

Fixed by dropping the check, and both the tooltip and the manual now say which
it is.

### 5. The rulers had no size of their own

`align-self: stretch` does not apply to a replaced element with an intrinsic
aspect ratio, and an `<svg>` with a viewBox has one. The horizontal ruler took
its height from that ratio and drew a 551 px strip down the middle of the
drawing. Found and fixed during the build, before the commit; recorded here
because the cause is not guessable from the symptom and the next person to put
an SVG in a grid cell will meet it again.

### 6. A `let` read from sixty lines above its declaration

`main.ts` reads `rulerAt` inside the store subscriber, and declared it after.
That is a temporal dead zone: the only reasons it was not a blank page and a
`ReferenceError` are that `showRulers` defaults to false, so the argument is
never evaluated, and that nothing notifies the store between the two lines.
Two accidents holding up one line. The declaration moved above the subscriber.

## Documentation claims

### 7. "The numbers along the edge fall on lines drawn across the canvas"

`ARCHITECTURE.md` §31, and false twice over: defects 2 and 3 above both broke it.
True now, and there is a browser check under it at six zoom levels rather than
one, because a single reading passes over a ladder that quantises.

### 8. "53 of 115 controls under 44 px"

`CLAUDE.md` quoted this as the measured basis for the mobile rule, and the method
behind it was never recorded, so it could not be checked or reproduced. A count
taken today with a stated method gives **71 of 72**. The two are not comparable
and no conclusion should be drawn from the difference.

Fixed by writing the method down as `tools/touch.mjs` and pointing `CLAUDE.md` at
it, with an instruction to re-run rather than trust the figure. The tool measures
the element that takes the press, which for a checkbox is its label and not the
13 px box.

Two claims in the same sentence were checked and stand: the rail covers the
canvas below 860 px (`styles.css`, the `max-width: 860px` query), and there is no
pinch to zoom on a touchscreen. Pinch on a trackpad was not tested.

### 9. "`rulerTicksFor` keeps ticks at least 7 px apart, so a 4000 px strip can hold about 570"

A comment in `rulers.ts`, stale the moment defect 3 was fixed. Now 9 px and about
440.

### 10. `guideKey` was exported and used nowhere

Not a claim so much as a promise: an export says something else needs this.
Removed.

## Tests that could not fail

Eleven mutations were applied to the new model code, one at a time, and the whole
suite run against each. Nine went red. The two that did not are below.

### 11. `rulerTicksFor` had no test at all

Deleting the line that consults the grid left the suite green. The function's
entire purpose is that ruler ticks agree with grid lines.

### 12. The test written to close 11 could not fail either

The first version compared `rulerTicksFor(1, ...)` against `gridDisplayFor(1, ...)`.
At a snap step of 1 the grid's answer and the bare ladder's are the same at every
zoom, so the assertion passed whether or not the grid was consulted. Rewritten at
a snap step of **3**, which is not on the 1-2-5 ladder, so the two disagree and
the assertion has something to say. That rewrite is what surfaced defects 2 and 3.

`view/rulers.ts` still has no unit test. It is DOM and measurement all the way
down, and what stands under it is the `guides` browser scenario, which now checks
both rulers against the drawn grid at six zoom levels. Recorded rather than
pretended away.

## What was checked and found sound

- **The snapping resolver**, after three rounds of change. Keylines and guides
  join the tier their dimension puts them in; the drawing still wins inside a
  tier; a switched-off tier is not answered by a guide. Ten tests across
  `keylines.test.ts` and `guides.test.ts`, and both files go red when the
  wiring is removed.
- **Guides in the history**, including that the snapshot copies the positions
  rather than sharing the array -- the store mutates in place, so a shared array
  would have the undo entry follow the drag it is meant to undo.
- **The keyline ratios**, against the published 24dp grid, and the export, which
  cannot carry a keyline or a guide because neither is in `Doc`.
- **All 32 browser scenarios**, and the 26 that predate this work still pass
  after the drawing surfaces moved into `.stage`.

One local-only failure, not a defect: `chrome` fails on this machine with the
Edge binary and passes in CI with Chrome, at the check that a focused checkbox
carries `aria-describedby`. A synthetic `pointerover` sets it correctly, so the
harness browser is not delivering the event to a 13 px target.
