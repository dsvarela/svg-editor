## Code that does nothing

### 1. Three checks written while blaming the wrong thing

Offsetting left 1.14 of error on a notched shape. Three separate additions were
made to fix it, each plausible:

- a break in a run wherever two surviving samples were far apart in space;
- a validity check on the corner computed between two runs;
- a distance check on that same corner.

The error stayed at **exactly 1.1349** through all three. That constancy is the
finding: an error that does not move under three changes is not being caused by
any of them. It was in `NearMap`, which used one number for its grid cells and
for its source polyline -- so the change that made queries cheap made the
polyline coarse, and a forty-unit edge got seven points along it. Separating the
two numbers took the error to 0.0028.

Removing all three additions afterwards changes **nothing**: every measurement
on every fixture is identical, all 726 tests pass, and the browser scenarios
pass. Two of them are now gone. The third is kept -- joining two runs at an
arbitrarily distant point is not worth leaving unguarded -- and the comment says
outright that nothing distinguishes it.

The lesson is cheap to state and was expensive here: **when a number does not
move, stop changing the thing you think produced it and check the thing that
measured it.**

## A claim that was wrong twice

### 2. "The chrome failure is the harness, not the app"

Recorded twice, the second time in the review of 2026-08-13, on the strength of
a synthetic `pointerover` working. It was a real accessibility defect: focusing a
control below the fold scrolls the panel, `scroll` was wired straight to `hide`,
and the scroll beat the tooltip's own timer -- so tabbing through a panel
described only what was already on screen. Fixed, and the reversal is in
`ARCHITECTURE.md` §38 rather than edited into the review it corrects.

Worth naming the shape of the mistake, because it is the same shape as finding 1:
a synthetic event proving the handler sound is evidence about the handler, not
about the path the real event takes. Both times the evidence answered a
neighbouring question.

## Tests that could not fail

Seven mutations were applied to the newest code, one at a time, with the whole
suite run against each. Three went red. The four that did not:

### 3. A stroke's closed-path side count is unchecked

`strokeOutline` refuses a closed path whose offsets come apart, and removing that
guard leaves the suite green -- the fixture written for it (a width of 60 on a
40-unit square) is refused earlier, by the offset returning nothing at all. The
guard is right and the test does not reach it.

### 4. The import path's "draws nothing" refusal has no unit test

Only the `importFile` browser scenario covers it. That is defensible -- the
refusal lives in `main.ts`, which has no unit tests at all -- and it is recorded
rather than left to be discovered.

### 5 and 6. Two checks in `meetAt`, covered above

Both removed. The remaining one is undistinguished and says so.

## What was checked and found sound

- **The offset**, on five shapes: exact to 0.0006 inward on a square, 0.0028 on a
  notched shape that separates into two paths, nothing at all when the distance
  consumes the shape. Removing the distance filter fails four tests.
- **Stroke to path**: two contours wound opposite ways on a closed path, one
  capped loop on an open one, and the cap arc's subdivision is load-bearing --
  one cubic per half turn fails a test.
- **All 43 browser scenarios**, including `chrome`, which passes locally for the
  first time.
- **The shopping list against the code**: 47 entries, 47 done, no partials, and
  no `TODO` or `FIXME` anywhere in `src`, `test` or `tools`.

One number in `CLAUDE.md` was stale by construction: "71 of 72 controls under
44 px" was measured before this stretch added the Angles, Guides, palette, File
and node-stepping controls. Re-measured with `node tools/touch.mjs`: **84 of 85**.
Thirteen controls arrived and the ratio did not move, which is the honest reading
-- the rule about 44 px is about new controls being laid out at that size, and
nothing built this stretch was.

That the number could be re-measured at all in the time it took to ask is the
whole return on writing the method down last time.
