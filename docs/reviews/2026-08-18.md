# Review, 2026-08-18

Covers the three commits that had had no review: the palette meeting the
contrast floors, the switch to Firefox, and the three browser scenarios that
went red on it.

**What it found: 1 check that could not fail, 2 scenarios that asserted nothing,
1 defect in the editor, 1 instrument that had never measured what it claimed,
and 5 comments describing code that is gone.** The check that could not fail is
the finding that matters, because it is the mechanism added to make exactly that
impossible.

---

## The check written to catch a test that cannot fail could not fail

`--audit` exists because thirteen scenarios once drove the editor, read the page
and asserted nothing, and nothing said so. It refuses that now by looking for a
call to `check` in each scenario's source.

It looked for `/\bcheck\(/`.

`await page.check('#filled')` is Playwright ticking a checkbox. It appears in
scenarios all through `tools/drive.mjs`, and the word boundary before `check`
falls after the dot, so it matches. Two scenarios satisfied the audit on it and
asserted nothing:

- **`combine`** ran all four boolean operations, recorded a status line, a fill,
  a shape list and a `d` string for each, undid between them, and returned the
  lot. Breaking `exclude` to compute a union changed four strings in the printed
  JSON and nothing else.
- **`makeOneShape`** placed two squares, made them one, switched the fill rule,
  split them again, and sampled `isPointInFill` at the centre and between the
  squares at every step. Every reading was returned and none compared.

`tsc` had been reporting both, as an unused `check` parameter, for as long as
they had existed. The diagnostic was visible in the editor the whole time.

The test is `/(?<![.\w])check\(/` now: `check(` with no dot or word character in
front of it. The two scenarios assert what their own comments had been claiming.

**`combine` measures enclosed area**, sampled with `isPointInFill` at the centre
of every unit square, which is exact for these axis-aligned polygons. Two 20 by
20 squares overlapping in a 10 by 10 corner give 700, 300, 100 and 600 for unite,
subtract, intersect and exclude. No two share a number, and a `d` string cannot
say this: four operations that produced the same shape would still print four
different strings if the contours came out in a different order.

**`makeOneShape` asserts the hole.** Same element and same geometry under both
rules, the centre painted under nonzero and not under evenodd, with the point
between the squares as the control -- without it a shape that vanished entirely
would read as a hole.

Both were watched failing. `exclude` computing a union reports `enclosed 700
square units, want 600`; the fill-rule button ignoring evenodd reports `evenodd
painted the middle, so there is no hole`.

## One press in six on a selected edge did the opposite

Rounding the harness's pointer to whole pixels broke `measureReadout`, and the
break was the find.

The controller reads a press by asking what is under it and looking for a
`data-hit` attribute. Without one the press starts a marquee, which clears the
selection. `.handle-line` carried no `data-hit` and no `pointer-events: none`,
and a latent handle's line runs from a node along the straight segment it would
bend -- directly over that segment's outline.

Sampled at every whole pixel along the top edge of a selected rectangle:

| Aim lands on | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| the outline | 79.5% | 95.9% |
| a latent handle line, which does nothing | 16.4% | 0% |
| a real control: bend, in, out | 4.0% | 4.0% |

**16.4% of the pixels along the edge deselected the shape instead of moving it.**
The line is dashed and one pixel wide, so nobody aiming at the edge could see why
the edge sometimes failed. It had survived because the harness was asking for
fractional pointer positions that stepped over it, and because a real user who
misses once simply presses again.

The rule is §54 of `ARCHITECTURE.md` now, and the audit every scenario runs
fails on any element that breaks it. Restoring the defect fails 31 of the 52.

Its second finding was `.guide`, which was swallowing nothing: its hit strip is
eight pixels wide and painted immediately after it, covering it everywhere. That
is another element's geometry rather than a rule, and geometry changes. Declared.

## Three scenarios failed on the engine, and none of them for the reason it looked

All three read as Firefox behaving differently from Chromium. All three were
defects in the test.

- **`backdrop`'s fixture PNG was truncated.** Its IDAT held 20 bytes of a deflate
  stream that needed more, so only the first of three rows could be
  reconstructed. Chromium decodes what arrived and reports the header's 4 by 3;
  Firefox refuses the image. **Every check passed on both engines**, because they
  all read the `<image>` element's attributes and none read a pixel. The fixture
  is built by the `png()` helper already in the file, and the check reads the far
  corner, which catches the old bytes on either engine.
- **`angles` asked for the pointer at client pixel 656.887.** A browser delivers
  whole pixels: Firefox truncates the fraction and Chromium keeps it, so the two
  put the pointer in different places and every assertion about sub-pixel
  geometry inherited the difference. `toClient` rounds now, and `toDoc` inverts
  it so a scenario computes its expectation from where the pointer is rather than
  where it aimed. The constant 49.7 was only ever right on one engine.
- **`traceWorker` measured main-thread blocking with a `longtask`
  `PerformanceObserver`.** `longtask` is a Chromium entry type, and `observe`
  ignores a type it does not know rather than refusing it -- so it read 0 ms for
  both runs and the scenario compared two numbers that had measured nothing. A
  10 ms interval measuring its own lateness reads 800 ms on a thread blocked for
  800, on both engines, over an 11 ms idle floor.

The `traceWorker` threshold moved from 400 to 450 because the instrument changed,
not because the page did: a timer gap spans consecutive tasks where a long-task
entry is one of them, so the same page reads higher. 274, 310 and 353 over three
runs. `ARCHITECTURE.md` §28's table says which instrument its figures came from
now, because the two are not comparable and 215 against 310 reads as a
regression that did not happen.

## `keys.mjs` had never measured Tab

Its reset between passes was `document.body.focus()`. The body is not focusable,
so the call moves nothing and each sweep resumed from wherever the
group-opening clicks had left focus, in the middle of the rail.

**Chromium wraps the tab order at the end of the page**, so 260 presses came
round to the top anyway and the count read 0 by luck. Firefox stops at the last
element, and everything before the resume point counted as unreachable: it
reported 12 controls that were fine. A temporary `tabindex` makes the body a real
focus target. This is the third instrument in this repository caught reporting a
number it was not measuring.

## Comments describing code that is gone

Five, all written within two days of the review, all by the same hand that then
reviewed them. Each named a past implementation rather than a present
constraint: the backdrop fixture, the traceWorker instrument, the angles
expectation, the `keys.mjs` reset, and the `browser.mjs` header.

`comments/check.mjs --only history` caught **none of the five**. It found eight
elsewhere in `tools/`, all older. Its rule 4 recall on fresh material written by
someone who knows the rule is worth what that suggests, and the ruleset says so
in its own words: it knows a list of phrasings, which is not the same as knowing
when a comment narrates a past.

The same pass found the checker's `--check` flag was not a flag it has. It
accepted it and ran the three defaults, and reported that as the answer. Fixed in
the handbook, along with the same hole in all six of its checkers.

## Smaller

- **`ENGINE` was exported from `browser.mjs` and nothing imported it.** Rule 13.
- **The swallower audit keyed on a hand-written list of ten tag names**, which a
  new kind of overlay element would join without anyone remembering. It asks
  `SVGGeometryElement` and its three siblings now, so the browser maintains the
  membership.
- **`launch()` called `process.exit(2)`.** It sits outside drive's top-level
  `try`, so nothing depended on that. A function whose type says it returns a
  browser leaves nothing for a caller wanting to try another engine, and no way
  to test this one.
- **`export const URL` was a string shadowing the global `URL` class** in all
  three importing tools. Harmless only because every `URL.createObjectURL` in
  `drive.mjs` runs inside `page.evaluate`, in the browser rather than in Node.
  `APP_URL` now, which is what the environment variable was called anyway.
- **`.shapelist .swatch` kept `var(--line)`** while six sibling borders moved to
  `--edge`. It is the same case and the clearest one: the swatch is filled with
  the shape's own colour, so a white shape on a white row is its border and
  nothing else. `--edge` is 1.61:1 on `--accent`, so a selected row takes the ink
  that row already proves.
- **Why `--edge` and `--line` are separate** was written only in
  `src/ui/pairs.txt`, a file the contrast checker reads. It is at the top of
  `styles.css` now, where somebody choosing a border colour is looking.
- **A process rule that is not written down.** A full sweep was started and then
  `tools/drive.mjs` was edited while it ran. Every scenario is a fresh `node
  tools/drive.mjs`, so the run was measuring a moving tree and its results were
  discarded. `CLAUDE.md` states this for `src/` and not for `tools/`.
